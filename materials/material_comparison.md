# Material Selection Summary

Selected via Granta EduPack Level 3 Eco Design database, using a
Fracture Toughness vs. Density Ashby chart methodology, filtered against
component-specific density, toughness, temperature, and price constraints.

## Main shell — PP (homopolymer, 30% glass fibre)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 0.0403 – 0.041 lb/in³ |
| Fracture toughness | 2.71 – 4.96 ksi·√in |
| Price | 0.767 – 0.916 USD/lb |

**Justification:** Best combination of low density and high fracture
toughness among candidates passing the shell's constraint set — outperformed
all other options on both axes simultaneously.

## Sensor detection window — PP (homopolymer, clarified/nucleated)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 0.0325 – 0.0328 lb/in³ |
| Fracture toughness | 2.09 – 2.2 ksi·√in |
| Price | 1.01 – 1.3 USD/lb |

**Justification:** Lowest density among viable candidates, prioritising
radiation transparency at the detection face, while retaining adequate
toughness for a thin (1.5mm) protective panel.

## Threaded USB-C cap — PP molding compound (40% random glass mat)

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Density | 0.0426 – 0.0434 lb/in³ |
| Fracture toughness | 12.6 – 15.5 ksi·√in |
| Price | 1.63 – 1.89 USD/lb |

**Justification:** Highest fracture toughness among realistic, non-directional
candidates — suited to a small part enduring repeated hand-tightening and
occasional drops.

## Note

All three selected materials are from the polypropylene family, differing
only in reinforcement type/level — a deliberate, consistent material
strategy across the enclosure rather than unrelated material choices per
component.
